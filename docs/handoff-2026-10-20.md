  (`scripts/slides_band_rotation.py`, notebook section 6g). Per case one
  Q figure and one U figure, each with the total parent-channel
  pseudo-Stokes (I-only sky; plus unrotated WMAP polarization; plus

  `notebooks/slides_pixel_sum/14_band_rotation_{f0.0002,uniform0.005}_{Q,U}.png`;

  polarized term alone (the rotated sky has I = 0), 1–2 % of beam-weighted
  I and below the leakage everywhere.
- **The two fig 14 cases are brackets, not a result and a correction**: the
  RM map × 0.0002 keeps the column's angular pattern across the beam and
  gives a stretched speckle (with the beam fixed, P(λ²; f) = P(fλ²; 1), so
  the 10–50 MHz band at f = 0.0002 is a 26 kHz stretch of the f = 1 ripple
  at 30 MHz); the uniform 0.005 rad/m² depth has no angular structure and
  tracks the unrotated term within 30 % above 28 MHz. The real local medium
  is between, and that is an ISM prior, not a map product. The earlier
  "rotates coherently" title and the receiver-surprise framing are gone.
- **Pseudo-Stokes heads-up figure for the collaboration**
  (`scripts/pseudo_stokes_leakage.py compute|plot`,
  `report/figures/pseudo_stokes_leakage.png`, cache
  `generated_data/cache/pseudo_stokes_leakage.npz`). A uniform unpolarized
  sky gives pseudo-Q and pseudo-U of a few per cent of I with sign changes
  across the band in every basis (raw E−W/N−S, gains-only, orthonormalized),
  so the sign changes in the leakage are the beam's off-zenith N–S/E–W
  pattern difference drifting with frequency, not the calibration. The
  orthonormalization reduces |Q| and |U| below ~34 MHz but makes U worse
  than raw above 38 MHz (−6 to −10 % vs 0 to +5 %). Decision: keep the
  orthonormalized basis (a fixed, invertible linear map of the 16 channels;
  model-perfect, no calibration error included).
