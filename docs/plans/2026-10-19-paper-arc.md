# The paper arc: sub-map structure, emitting depth, form and range

Status: agreed 2026-10-19, supersedes the phase order in
[the framing plan](2026-10-17-paper-framing.md#plan-to-the-paper) and the
status text of [the implementation plan](2026-10-17-paper-implementation.md).
The physics and the code of both documents stand. What changes is the
order in which the paper and the group talk present them, and one missing
scenario.

## Why the order changes

The 2026-08-18 audit showed that the pixel sum does not converge because the
surviving polarization is set by Faraday-depth structure below the map's
resolution. The licensed next step is: any prediction is a model of that
sub-map structure, so state a family of models and report the range.

The 2026-10-17 review then argued that depth depolarization confines the
emission seen below 50 MHz to a few parsecs, so the column screen is not
the screen LuSEE looks through. That is a separate physical argument. It
does not follow from the non-convergence, and the implementation on
`implementation/depth-depolarization` replaced the column anchor with
assumed local parameters (dispersion 0.002 and 0.02 rad/m², 5 pc path)
without carrying the column screen along as a scenario. The talk and the
paper therefore read as a jump from "the sum failed" to "a new local
model". The fix is to present the work as a nested bracket:

1. the column screen with stated sub-map structure (the old approach, done
   as an ensemble);
2. the emitting depth as a second axis, at the same anchor;
3. the local-medium case as the far end of that axis, where the anchor
   itself is unknown.

Every model in the bracket predicts the same form. They differ in
amplitude by orders of magnitude, and that range is the result.

### 2. The answer depends on structure the map lacks: make it a family

### 3. The column screen through the real instrument

Add the column-anchored screens to the `screens` list of
`configs/paper/covariance.json`, with `sigma_depth` of order 10,
`mean_depth` the beam-weighted column, `theta0_rad` from Delta phi at 1°
by the pilot's relation, and the three alphas, and run the spin-2
real-instrument covariance and mixed-depth stages. The pipeline accepts
this screen model unchanged (`spin_covariance.ScreenModel`, alpha ≤ 2).

What to watch: at 30 MHz the coherence angle of the column screen is
between 1e-7 and 4e-4 rad across the family (ell_c from 2e3 to 1e7), above
the stage's `max_degree` of 4096. The beam reads below ell 100 and the
rotated spectrum is white up to ell_c, so the truncation should not matter
for the beam-weighted power; the angular quadrature is log-spaced from
1e-10 rad, so the core is sampled. The stage's gates (refinement doubling,
lmax 64 cut, 1 % relative tolerance) decide, and the branch refuses rather
than approximates, so the outcome is either a converged number or a
refusal that names the limit. If refused, report the pilot's white-floor
number with the spin-2 correction bounded by the scalar-versus-all-pair
comparison already recorded.

Column-screen parameters for the config (sigma 10 rad/m², chord at 1°
anchor): theta0 in rad for (alpha, Delta phi at 1°) = 0.82 (2, 0.3),
0.25 (2, 1), 0.081 (2, 3), 1.8 (5/3, 0.3), 0.42 (5/3, 1), 0.11 (5/3, 3);
for alpha = 1 the fitted theta0 exceeds the sphere (0.38 to 39 rad) and the
model is only meaningful through its small-separation behaviour.

Pin: the pilot's beam-weighted surviving power at 30 MHz, 9e-8 of the
unrotated value for the baseline, recovered within the scalar-to-spin-2
difference.

Done 2026-10-19 (`configs/paper/covariance-column.json`,
`docs/faraday-covariance-column-results.json`): six scenarios, alpha 2 and
5/3 at anchors 0.3, 1, 3 rad/m², all twelve cases pass the gates at

The smooth term is exactly zero. Parent-channel surviving power is 0.61

### 4. The emitting depth as a second axis

Screen fraction f in {0, 0.6, 1}, uniform slab and Gaussian profile, as
already implemented. Present two knobs separately:

- **Where the emission sits**, at the column anchor of step 3. The delta
  screen at any f is closed-form; the slab and Gaussian at a column of
  Phi lambda² near 1e3 rad will likely exceed the depth quadrature's
  maximum order and be refused. A refusal is reported as such, not
  worked around.
- **How much depth structure is inside the horizon**, the existing
  local-dispersion scenarios (0.002 and 0.02 rad/m²), which are the far
  end of the axis. The horizon table from the review (0.3 to 7 pc) is the
  reason the far end exists, stated as a reason and not as a verdict on
  step 3.

Code: `scripts/depth_depolarization.py`, `scripts/mixed_depth_predictions.py`
and their records. State: exists for the local scenarios; the column rows
are new runs.

Done 2026-10-19, analytically. The mixed-depth stage refuses the column
screens before any profile runs (`DepthConvergenceError`: the Hermite depth
expansion needs more than `max_degree` 4096 at a column phase of 1e3 rad;
`configs/paper/mixed-depth-column.json` records the attempt). In the
saturated regime the two closed forms suffice:

- **Delta screen at fraction f.** The rotation is exp(2i f lambda^2 Phi), so
  the result is the full-column screen with the anchor scaled to f times
  Delta phi at 1 degree, and the surviving power scales as f^(-4/alpha).
  Checked on both the scalar pilot and the real-instrument column run: the
  anchor ratios 0.3 to 1 and 1 to 3 give power ratios 18.0 and 14.3 for
  alpha 5/3 (predicted 18.0, 14.0) and 11.1 and 9.2 for alpha 2 (11.1, 9.0).
  A screen at f = 0.6 therefore gives 2.8 (alpha 2) to 3.4 (alpha 5/3)
  times the f = 1 power; f = 0.3 gives 11 to 18 times.
- **Emission mixed through the column (uniform slab).** Each sightline's
  complex amplitude is e^(i x Phi) sinc(x Phi) with x = lambda^2, so its
  magnitude is of order 1/(x |Phi|): median 3e-4 at 30 MHz over the
  Hutschenreuter map, 8e-4 at 50 MHz. The sky-rms power factor is 5e-4
  (30 MHz) and 1.3e-3 (50 MHz), carried by the sightlines whose column
  depth passes near zero. The slab speckle is therefore three orders of
  magnitude below the screen speckle in power, and any emission spread over
  more than about 0.01 rad/m^2 of depth at 30 MHz (0.03 at 50 MHz) is
  depth-depolarized on its sightline; a truncated Gaussian profile is the
  same order through its edges.

So the column-anchor f-axis spans a factor 3 upward (screen at 0.6) to a
factor 1e3 downward (uniform slab) from the f = 1 screen, and the emission
that survives mixing at 30 MHz is that within 0.01 rad/m^2 of a fixed
depth, which is the Faraday-horizon statement seen from the column side.
The local scenarios on the branch are the far end of this axis.

### 5a. Decision table and the Stokes I leak (done 2026-10-19)

`scripts/column_decision.py` -> `docs/column-decision-results.json`: the
column-screen speckle at the WMAP-K brightness against the measured-system
reference receiver noise (3 nV/rtHz, 0.1 pA/rtHz, both in the shared 1 K
enclosure kelvin) at one sidereal phase for 1, 12, 24 and 48 lunations of
actual-site exposure (0.3, 2.2, 4.5, 9.0 h). At 30 MHz, 24 lunations: the
smoothest, lowest-anchor case (alpha 2, 0.3 rad/m^2) reaches speckle/noise
1.9 in the parent channel and a 4 sigma variance excess over the simulated
band; the anchor-1 cases reach 0.2 to 0.6 and below 1 sigma; alpha 1 is
below 1e-2. Doubling to 48 lunations raises these by root 2 in amplitude and
2 in variance significance. For every scenario but the most optimistic the
outcome is a bound.

The Faraday polarized sky leaks into the four-port pseudo-Stokes I at 0.8 of
its Q amplitude (the zenith calibration cannot make pseudo-I polarization-
blind off zenith): 0.05 to 0.1 K at 30 MHz for the column screen, 2e-6 of
the sky, with the same 11 kHz coherence. Averaged over a 1 MHz 21-cm channel
and the lunar day this is a few mK of non-smooth residual against a 20 to
40 mK dark-ages signal; the unrotated polarization's leak into I is
hundreds of kelvin but spectrally smooth.

### 5. Form and range

The manifestation is the same in every scenario: a speckle that appears as
fixed-pattern excess variance in parent channels at and below 30 MHz, is
resolved in zoom bins, repeats with the sidereal day, and does not
integrate down with time. The amplitude is a range across the bracket,
reported against I to Q,U leakage (parent-bin Q/I of 0.04) and against the
loaded radiometer noise at 1 and 24 lunations.

Code: `scripts/measured_system.py`, `scripts/residual_signal.py`,
`scripts/faraday_separability.py`, `scripts/polarized_brightness.py`.
State: exist for the local scenarios; each new scenario row flows through
them. Talk deliverable: one table (scenario, 30 and 50 MHz speckle RMS,
ratio to leakage, ratio to noise) and one figure of simulated parent and
zoom spectra.

### 6. Inference, last

With the forward machinery in hand, explore estimators for the speckle
against the leakage residual. The lesson from PR 8 stands: the toy
inference failed on nuisance degeneracies, so the realistic version lives
or dies on separating the speckle from the smooth leakage residual. This is
after the manuscript's prediction sections, not before.

## Caveats the paper carries

- The column screen is the full Galactic column; the emission may sit
  anywhere along it, which is the step 4 axis.
- The intrinsic polarized sky is WMAP K extrapolated with beta = -2.8, with
  an assumed polarized fraction and an assumed turnover below 20 MHz.
- The Faraday sky is the Hutschenreuter posterior mean, smoother than the
  sky and uninformative below its resolution.
- The sub-map structure function is assumed; alpha and the one-degree
  anchor are the parameters of that assumption.
- The beam is an electromagnetic model, not a measurement; the as-built,
  C4-symmetrized and diagonal-ZA variants give the model spread.
- Receiver noise, exposure and the lunar surface model are as stated in
  the records; none is measured yet.
