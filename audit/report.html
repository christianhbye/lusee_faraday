Numerical audit · lusee_faraday

The diffuse-sky ripple is the pixel grid

The delay-space Faraday signature in Steps 2–4 is a histogram of the RM map, not a measurement of the sky. Seven tests, a derivation, and a positive control that shows the tests have power.

Branch croissant-crosscheck Band tested 30 & 50 MHz Maps faraday2020v2 · WMAP K Date 2026-08-18

1What is and isn’t affected

The claim is narrow. Most of the pipeline is untouched.

The finding applies to one quantity: the beam-integrated polarization of the diffuse sky after passing through a spatially structured Faraday screen. Everything that does not involve a resolved RM gradient is fine.

PROGRESS.md already reached the right qualitative conclusion — “the observed P through the Faraday screen is leakage-dominated”. That survives, and gets stronger. What does not survive is the residual quoted as the sky-polarization contribution (|dP|/I ≈ 1.7e-4) and the amplitude of the Step-4 delay spectrum.

2The mechanism

One number decides it: phase change per pixel.

Faraday rotation multiplies the polarized sky by exp(2i φ(n̂) λ²). On faraday2020v2 the rms gradient is 4328 rad m−2 per radian. At 30 MHz, λ² = 99.9 m², so the polarization angle winds by

2 · 4328 · 99.9 ≈ 8.6 × 105 rad per radian on the sky ≈ 1730 radians between adjacent pixels at nside = 512

Nyquist needs less than π per pixel. We are over by a factor of ~550. Sampling that integrand gives a random walk, not a quadrature.

The same statement in delay space is more useful. The RMSF of the ±0.1 MHz band has FWHM δφ = 2√3 / Δλ² = 2.60 rad m−2. A delay cell of that width corresponds to a level set of the RM map about 1 arcmin thick — against 7 arcmin pixels at nside 512. Step one pixel in any direction and RM moves ~4 rad m−2, three cells over. So consecutive grid pixels essentially never land in the same delay cell, and each cell gets filled by pixels scattered incoherently across the whole sky.

Consequence Each delay cell sums pixels that are spatially unrelated. The sum is a random walk over pixels, whose magnitude is set by the pixel count — a property of the grid, not the sky.

3What random phases predict

If the mechanism above is right, these formulae should hold exactly. They do.

Write the beam-integrated sky polarization on a pixel grid, with the beam normalization folded into the weights. This is the P(λ²) of RM synthesis — the Fourier partner of the Faraday dispersion function F(φ) used below:

P(λ²) = Σp wp e 2iφpλ²,    wp = Bp(Q+iU)p ⁄ Σq Bq
Notation P(λ²) here is the beam-integrated complex linear polarization of the sky, Q+iU — not Stokes V, and not the observed pseudo-Stokes P of the instrument, which is leakage-dominated. It is the sky-polarization term alone, which is exactly the term under audit.

Expanding the modulus squared separates diagonal from cross terms:

⟨|P|²⟩ = Σp |wp|²  +  Σp≠q wpwq* ⟨e 2i(φp−φq)λ²⟩

When the phases decorrelate the cross terms average away and only the diagonal survives:

⟨|P|²⟩ → Σp |wp|² the incoherent, or shot-noise, floor

What “pixelization shot noise” means here

Nothing in this simulation is random — run it twice and it returns the same number. The name is borrowed from the 1/n̄ discreteness term in a galaxy power spectrum, and it fits for the same reason: a positive, additive floor set purely by sampling density, carrying no information about the field’s correlations.

The mechanism is a random walk. The physical quantity is a continuum integral ∫ w(n̂) e2iφ(n̂)λ² dΩ; the code evaluates a Riemann sum over pixels. That sum converges only if the phase changes slowly between neighbouring samples. Here it moves ~1700 rad per pixel, so every pixel contributes a step of length |wp| in an arbitrary direction, and the total is a two-dimensional random walk whose expected squared length is the sum of squared step lengths — the floor derived above. Because the beam normalization makes wp ∝ 1/Npix across Npix terms, refining the grid shrinks the answer: more steps, each smaller.

So the quantity is deterministic but is fixed by an arbitrary numerical knob rather than by physics. Its three fingerprints, each tested in §4:

  • Magnitude matches the random-walk prediction Σp|wp|²;
  • Scaling follows 1/Npix, a factor 4 per nside doubling;
  • Indifference to structure — a random walk does not care what order its steps come in, so shuffling the map changes nothing essential.

A converged physical integral fails all three: it settles to a constant under refinement and separates from its shuffle without bound. It is not instrumental noise, not Monte Carlo noise, and not primarily WMAP’s map noise — that contributes too, but smoothing to 10° removes it and the floor persists (test 05).

Two consequences follow immediately, and both are testable.

It must scale as 1/Npix

Each wp carries a factor 1/ΣBq ∝ 1/Npix, and there are Npix terms, so Σ|wp|² ∝ 1/Npix. A genuine sky integral converges to a constant; this falls by 4× per nside doubling.

The delay spectrum must be the RM histogram

Applying the window hk and Parseval to the discrete transform,

Σφ|F(φ)|² = N Σk|Pkhk|²  ⇒  ⟨Σφ|F|²⟩ = N (Σkhk2) Σp|wp|²

and, resolving by delay,

⟨|F(φ)|²⟩ = Σp |wp|²   W(φ − 2φp) the |w|²-weighted histogram of the RM map, convolved with the RMSF

Note what the weighting is: |wp|², a one-point statistic. Two skies with identical RM histograms but different |w|²–RM correlations give different spectra — which is why the real map and its shuffle do not lie on top of each other in panel A. Neither version encodes the spatial coherence of the polarized emission, and that coherence is the entire content of a genuine ripple measurement.

This last identity is why the Step-4 result looked so convincing. Every reported feature is a property of the RM map alone:

  • “the envelope images the sky RM distribution” — it literally is the RM histogram;
  • “cutoff at φmax ≈ 2400” — a property of the RM map, and not even max(RM_map): the cutoff sits at whatever largest |RM| the beam actually sees. In the test geometry here, every pixel with |RM| > 2000 is below the horizon and the spectrum cuts off near 1720;
  • “ν−3 scaling verified” — that is the dλ²/dν Jacobian.

None of the three tests the polarized sky. Only the amplitude does, and the amplitude is 1/Npix.

4Evidence

Seven tests on the real data. Each has a control.

Maps
faraday2020v2.hdf5 (faraday_sky_mean, RING, nside 512) and WMAP K Q/U (reordered NEST→RING). Used at native resolution via their aℓm to ℓmax = 1023, so refining nside changes the quadrature without changing the fields.
Beam
A cos²θ lobe about a fixed galactic axis, zero below the horizon. Not the LuSEE response — see §6. Every test compares a measurement against a prediction built from the same weights, so the diagnostic does not depend on the beam shape.
Band
16384 channels of 25 kHz/2048 spanning ±0.1 MHz — the grid used in scripts/common.py. Centred at 30 MHz unless stated.
Time
A single time step. No sidereal rotation; the horizon cut is fixed.
Transform
Type-3 NUFFT over all pixels gives P(λ²); Hann window; FFT along λ². Note λ² runs backwards (it decreases as ν rises), so the recovered axis is −φ.

05

Search for coherent sky structure

MethodSmooth only the Q/U aℓm with Gaussian beams of FWHM 0/1/3/10°, leaving RM at native resolution, then repeat test 03. WMAP K per-pixel Q noise is 0.043 mK against a map rms of 0.079 mK (S/N 1.85), so 3° and 10° are firmly signal-dominated.

Unlike |P| at one frequency, the delay transform refocuses pixels of equal RM — so it can retain sky coherence. If real structure contributes, smoothing the polarized map to where WMAP K is signal-dominated must raise the ratio above 1.

Flat at 1.0 everywhere. No coherent excess at any scale.

06

Shuffled-RM control, with a positive control

MethodPermute RM values across pixel indices with a fixed seed: the histogram is preserved exactly, all spatial structure is destroyed, and w is untouched. Separately scale RM by f, which rescales the level-set band thickness by 1/f while leaving grid, band and delay resolution alone.

Permuting RM values across pixels preserves the histogram exactly and destroys all spatial structure. Scaling RM by 0.02 widens the level-set bands to 30 pixels, so that case is resolved and must behave differently.

Verdict Across the LuSEE band the diffuse Faraday delay spectrum is the pixelization shot noise of the sky grid. Its shape is the RM map’s histogram; its amplitude is set by Npix.

5Why brute force will not fix it

Three independent obstructions. Any one of them is sufficient.

The obvious response to §4 is to raise the resolution until the sum converges. It cannot be done, and even if it could, two further problems sit underneath that no amount of compute touches.

5.1 Resolution

Convergence needs the phase to move by less than π between adjacent samples: 2|∇φ| λ² θpix < π. Solving for each grid actually used:

nside 512 -> converged only for lam^2 < 0.182 m^2 (nu > 704 MHz) nside 1024 -> < 0.363 m^2 (nu > 497 MHz) nside 2048 -> < 0.726 m^2 (nu > 352 MHz)

These predict panel C quantitatively. At λ² = 0.14 every grid is inside its limit and all three agree to four digits; at λ² = 0.56 only nside 512 has crossed its threshold, and it is exactly the one that departs, by 11%.

Reaching the LuSEE band needs nside ≳ 2.8×105 at 30 MHz and 2.5×106 at 10 MHz — of order 1012 and 1014 pixels. And the payoff is negative: extrapolating the converged part of panel C down to the band suggests a true suppression near 10−4, at least ten times below the 2.4×10−3 the sim reports at nside 2048. You would spend 1012 pixels to find a number smaller than the artifact it replaces.

5.2 The Faraday screen is not determined

The observable depends on the screen only through e2iφλ², so an uncertainty δφ becomes a phase uncertainty 2 δφ λ². faraday2020v2 ships its own per-pixel error, and it is not small:

sigma p10 3.65 p50 9.82 p90 43.14 p99 136.58 rad/m^2 median sigma/|RM| = 0.50 sky fraction with sigma > |RM| : 24.6%

The typical pixel’s rotation measure is known to about a factor of two, and on a quarter of the sky the reconstruction does not even fix the sign. Propagated into phase:

band median phase uncertainty sky fraction exceeding one full turn 10 MHz 2810 turns 100% 30 MHz 312 turns 100% 50 MHz 112 turns 100% 100 MHz 28 turns 100% 400 MHz 1.8 turns 76% 800 MHz 0.4 turns 22%

The threshold 2σλ² = π sits at λ² = 0.16 m², i.e. 750 MHz. Below it the map stops constraining the polarization angle at all. Across the entire LuSEE band, 100% of the sky has a phase uncertainty exceeding a full turn — e2iφλ² is known only to lie somewhere on the unit circle. A perfect simulation of this map would be a perfect simulation of the wrong sky, and there is no better map: the uncertainty is a property of the reconstruction, not of the file format.

5.3 The polarized template is not determined either

The same argument applies independently to Q+iU. WMAP K polarization at nside 512:

per-pixel noise sigma 0.0429 mK (median) map rms 0.0750 mK noise-subtracted signal 0.0617 mK -> per-pixel S/N = 1.45 noise share of per-pixel variance: 32% per-mode signal drops below noise at ell ~ 418 (~0.4 deg) fraction of polarized variance above that ell: 30%

This matters here in a specific way. The floor of §3 is Σp|wp|² — a sum over per-pixel magnitudes, which weights every scale equally. Noise-dominated small scales therefore enter at full strength, and roughly a third of the floor is WMAP’s own instrument noise being Faraday-rotated. Test 05 shows this is not the whole story, since smoothing to 10° removes it and the floor persists — but part of what the pipeline propagates was never sky.

Compounding it, the sky model carries this map from 23 GHz to 30 MHz at β = −2.8, a factor of 1.2×108. Signal-to-noise survives a multiplicative rescaling untouched, so this does not make things worse — but it is worth stating plainly that the polarized sky being fed to the simulation is a hundred-million-fold extrapolation of a template whose per-pixel S/N is 1.45.

Taken together All three thresholds land between roughly 350 and 800 MHz, by three unrelated mechanisms — quadrature sampling, screen reconstruction error, and template noise. That is why panel C converges above ~800 MHz and degrades below ~400: it is where all three relax at once. Inside the LuSEE band the diffuse deterministic calculation is not expensive, it is ill-posed.

6What this does not establish

Stated plainly, because the next step depends on it.

  • The true ripple amplitude. The discrete estimate falls as 1/Npix while pixels are coarser than a coherence patch, then plateaus at the continuum value. These runs show you are in the falling regime everywhere in-band up to nside 2048; they do not locate the plateau.
  • The physics of the superposition. A broadband ripple from many superposed RMs is real physics. Nothing here says the effect does not exist — only that this calculation is not measuring it.
  • Beam realism. Tests used a cos² lobe, not the BGL_v16 four-port kernel. The shot-noise diagnostic is beam-independent by construction — it compares the measured power to Σ|wp|² for whatever weights are supplied — but confirming through the real response is cheap and worth doing.
  • The point-source result. Argued, not re-run: a single Faraday depth has no spatial gradient, so none of this applies. Steps 0–1 should be unaffected.
